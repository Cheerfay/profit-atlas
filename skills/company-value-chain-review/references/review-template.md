## 1. Input checklist

Useful inputs include:

- Company name and comparison set.
- Time period, defaulting to the latest three complete fiscal years.
- Annual reports or already completed financial analysis.
- A project, role, or internal workflow when the review will later connect to personal experience.

Do not delay analysis for optional inputs. Use reasonable defaults and state them.

## 2. Layer 1 question bank

### Customer and payment

- Who directly transfers money to the company?
- Is the direct payer the same as the end user or beneficiary?
- Is payment one-time, recurring, usage-based, transaction-based, risk-based, or financed?
- What problem is important enough that the customer pays rather than tolerates it?

### Revenue and profit

- Which business produces the largest revenue?
- Which produces the largest gross profit?
- Does a low-revenue business produce a disproportionate share of profit?
- Is an apparently high-margin business project-based, volatile, regulated, or customer-concentrated?
- Does the company depend on investment income, subsidies, fair-value gains, or tax benefits to report net profit?

### Cost and risk

- Which costs vary with volume?
- Which fixed costs must be absorbed regardless of sales?
- Which liabilities belong economically to suppliers, customers, policyholders, lenders, or future service obligations?
- What reverses when growth slows: inventory, receivables, supplier credit, customer advances, or capacity utilization?
- What single failure would most damage revenue, cost, trust, or solvency?

### One-sentence test

A strong sentence names:

1. The mechanism or capability.
2. The paying customer.
3. The customer's consequential problem.
4. The economic form of earnings.

Weak:

> The company provides innovative products and excellent services.

Strong:

> The company uses risk pricing and long-duration capital management to turn households' uncertain losses into standardized insurance contracts, earning underwriting and investment returns.

## 3. Layer 2 question bank

### Build the main chain

- What triggers the customer's need?
- How does the company acquire or reach the customer?
- Where is the product, risk, inventory, or service designed?
- What must be delivered or executed before revenue is earned?
- Where does quality, fraud, credit, compliance, or operational risk enter?
- When is revenue recognized and cash collected?
- What creates renewal, repurchase, referral, assets under management, or repeated transactions?

### Locate economic decision points

- Which step determines volume or conversion?
- Which step determines price or take rate?
- Which step determines unit cost or utilization?
- Which step creates future liabilities or loss exposure?
- Which step most affects trust and retention?
- Which step has the strongest data feedback loop?

### Keep the chain minimal

Combine steps when they do not change the economic interpretation. Split a step only when it independently determines revenue, cost, risk, or trust.

A useful chain usually has six to nine nodes.

## 5. Common analytical mistakes

- Describing a company's products instead of its economic mechanism.
- Treating the largest revenue source as the largest profit source.
- Calling an infrastructure or retention activity profitable merely because it is strategically important.
- Using net profit without removing material non-operating gains.
- Drawing an org chart instead of a value-to-cash workflow.
- Listing every process and losing the decisive chain.
- Assuming an internal workflow from public filings without marking it as inference.
- Claiming a project affects revenue when it only improves a local metric with no demonstrated linkage.
