# CATL: What the Business Lives On and How Value Becomes Cash

> **Report date:** 2026-10-22
> **Period:** FY2023–FY2025 (three complete fiscal years)
> **Scope:** Contemporary Amperex Technology Co., Limited (CATL), consolidated group unless a product line is explicitly named
> **Units:** RMB billions unless stated otherwise
> **Evidence labels:** **[Disclosed]** stated in a primary source; **[Calculated]** derived from disclosed figures; **[Inference]** business interpretation based on cited evidence.
> **Boundary:** Research example, not investment advice.

## Answer in one minute

CATL is paid mainly by automakers and energy-storage customers for battery systems, with smaller revenue from battery materials, recycling, mineral resources, and other activities. In FY2025, power-battery systems generated 74.7% of group revenue and storage systems 14.7%. This identifies the main revenue engine, but the company does not disclose net profit by product line; product gross margin is not segment net profit. **[Disclosed]**

The economic engine is the repeated conversion of battery chemistry, cell and pack engineering, manufacturing yield, and customer qualification into large-volume deliveries and collections. Scale and customer validation help sustain the business, while material costs, price competition, product performance, factory utilization, and global execution determine whether that scale earns attractive returns. **[Inference]**

### Keep group economics separate from power-battery economics

- CATL's group revenue includes power batteries, storage batteries, battery materials and recycling, mineral resources, and other business. Group revenue and group net profit cannot be attributed wholly to power batteries.
- Product-level gross margin is disclosed, but product-level operating profit and net profit are not. Do not rank product lines by net profitability from the disclosed gross margins alone.
- The 2025 power-battery system gross margin (23.84%) was nearly unchanged from 2024 (23.94%), while power-battery revenue rose 25.1%. This indicates higher sales with a similar gross-margin rate; it does not show the exact power-battery profit contribution after R&D, selling, administrative, financing, tax, or shared costs. **[Disclosed/Inference]**
- The consolidated figures include minority interests in some subsidiaries; attributable net profit is the amount belonging to parent-company shareholders. Cash flow is the consolidated group's operating cash flow. They are related but not interchangeable measures.

## Layer 2 — The main value-to-cash workflow

> Automaker or storage-project need → application and performance specification → chemistry/cell/pack or storage-system design → sourcing and scaled production → testing, qualification, and delivery → revenue recognition and customer collection → service, warranty support, and follow-on programs

This is a public-evidence-based synthesis, not a claim that CATL uses one uniform internal process for every customer or product. **[Inference]** The annual reports disclose product scope and revenue-recognition rules; the detailed sequencing of customer qualification, engineering, manufacturing release, and after-sales execution is not fully disclosed.

| Economic result | Decisive workflow step | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Customer program wins, delivery volume, product mix, and delivery acceptance | Power and storage systems account for most revenue; goods revenue is recognized when control transfers to the customer. [Disclosed/Inference] |
| Cost | Materials procurement, manufacturing yield, and capacity utilization | Direct materials were 71.79% of main-business battery cost in 2025; process yield and utilization affect cost absorption. The latter relationship is an operating inference. [Disclosed/Inference] |
| Risk | Product design validation, quality control, safe delivery, and supply continuity | A failure can affect customer programs, warranty exposure, regulatory standing, and future orders. [Inference] |
| Customer experience and trust | Product reliability, qualification evidence, delivery consistency, and after-sales support | Automakers integrate battery systems into vehicle platforms and depend on dependable supply and technical support. [Inference] |

## Core operating contradiction

CATL must make very large, continuing investments in technology, manufacturing capacity, and global supply to win customer programs and spread fixed costs across volume; yet rapid capacity growth can lower returns if demand, customer programs, yields, or utilization lag, while battery-price competition can pass input-cost declines through to buyers. FY2025 illustrates the tension: power-battery revenue grew 25.1%, but its gross margin was 23.84%, nearly flat with 23.94% in 2024; meanwhile group operating cash flow grew to RMB 133.2bn, and investing cash flow was RMB -94.5bn. **[Disclosed/Inference]** The investing cash-flow figure also includes investment purchases and should not be read as capital expenditure alone.

## Evidence limitations

1. Public reports do not provide product-line operating profit or net profit. This review therefore identifies disclosed product gross margins and group-level net profit separately.
2. Customer names are anonymized in the 2025 annual report's top-five customer table. The reported concentration is usable, but named customer dependence cannot be audited from that table.
3. The workflow is an economic map inferred from disclosed product, delivery, and accounting information; it is not a verified internal process map.
4. Cash conversion above compares operating cash flow with attributable net profit and excludes investing needs, acquisitions, and financing. It is not free cash flow or a measure of distributable cash.
5. FY2025 represents the latest complete fiscal year in this report; later-period performance is outside scope.
