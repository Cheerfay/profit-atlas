# China New-Energy Vehicle Value-Chain Profit Pool Test (2023–2025)

> Test date: 2026-10-22  
> Test purpose: Determine whether `industry-profit-pool-research` can reach evidence-bounded industry conclusions through its general workflow when no automotive-specific module exists  
> Scope: China's new-energy vehicle value chain, focused on the Chinese market while also considering export compliance; representative companies are CATL, BYD, Li Auto, and Fuyao Glass  
> Financial period: The three complete fiscal years from 2023 through 2025  
> Unit: RMB billions unless otherwise stated  
> Evidence labels: **[Disclosed]** directly reported by a primary source; **[Calculated]** derived from disclosed figures; **[Inference]** a judgment based on multiple pieces of evidence

[中文版本](automotive-profit-pool-test-result-zh.md)

## 1. Executive conclusions

1. **Rapid sales growth does not imply rich vehicle-manufacturing profits.** China NEV sales rose from 9.495 million vehicles in 2023 to 16.49 million in 2025, a two-year CAGR of about 31.8%. Yet under the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zwfwck/sjfb/202601/t20260127_1962382.html) definition, the entire automotive manufacturing industry generated approximately RMB 11.18 trillion in revenue and RMB 461.0 billion in total profit in 2025, implying a revenue-profit ratio of only about 4.1%. The latter scope covers both internal-combustion and new-energy vehicles, so it is not an NEV vehicle margin. It is still sufficient to show that industry scale and profit attractiveness are not the same thing. **[Disclosed/Calculated]**

2. **Stronger profit pools concentrate in critical systems protected by technology, qualification, scale, and customer lock-in rather than being evenly distributed across the chain.** In 2025, CATL reported RMB 423.7 billion in revenue, RMB 72.2 billion in net profit attributable to shareholders, RMB 133.2 billion in operating cash flow, and a 23.84% gross margin for power-battery systems. Its revenue was lower than BYD's, but its net profit and operating cash flow were higher. **[Disclosed]** This indicates that a leading battery-system supplier can build stronger profit quality through scale, R&D, customer validation, and supply-chain bargaining power; it does not mean every battery producer has equivalent economics. **[Inference]**

3. **Vehicle profits are highly dispersed, and product cycles plus price competition can rapidly consume cash.** BYD remained profitable through vertical integration and scale, but its 2025 attributable net profit fell 19% and operating cash flow declined from RMB 133.5 billion in 2024 to RMB 59.1 billion. Li Auto's 2025 revenue fell 22%, operating profit turned negative, and operating cash flow was negative RMB 8.6 billion. **[Disclosed]** Automakers control a large revenue pool, but R&D, distribution, capacity, inventory, warranties, and new-model cycles make that profit pool unstable. **[Inference]**

4. **Differentiated components can form profit pools that are smaller than vehicle assembly but economically superior to it.** Fuyao Glass is not a pure NEV company, but its automotive-glass business represents components protected by qualification barriers, global supply capability, and product upgrades. In 2025, the company reported RMB 45.8 billion in revenue, RMB 9.3 billion in attributable net profit, and RMB 12.1 billion in operating cash flow; group gross margin calculated as revenue minus operating cost was approximately 37.3%. **[Disclosed/Calculated]** This result cannot be generalized to all components, but it shows that “auto parts” is not one uniform economic model. **[Inference]**

5. **The most credible external startup positions are not another automaker, a generic autonomous-driving model, or a capital-heavy recycling plant, but cross-company compliance and evidence infrastructure.** Four wedges deserve priority testing: export-compliance and software-update evidence, battery identity and lifecycle data, charging-asset diagnostics and maintenance, and supplier-quality change tracking. All four cross company boundaries, benefit from neutral records, have recurring budget owners, and do not require a startup to own a vehicle brand or large manufacturing assets first. AI is usually an important enhancement rather than the source of entry rights. **[Inference]**

## 2. Market scale: NEV penetration approaches one half

| Metric | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| NEV sales (10,000 vehicles) | 949.5 | 1,286.6 | 1,649.0 |
| Year-on-year growth | 37.9% | 35.5% | 28.2% |
| Share of new-vehicle sales | Approx. 31.6% | 40.9% | 47.9% |

The 2023 sales figure comes from the [China Association of Automobile Manufacturers](https://en.caam.org.cn/Index/show/catid/66/id/2039.html); the 2024 figure appears in a [government disclosure](https://static.sse.com.cn/bond/bridge2/disclosure/announcement/c/202512/bb757b_20251218_V721.pdf) citing CAAM data; the 2025 figure comes from [Ministry of Commerce market information](https://cif.mofcom.gov.cn/cif/html/mobile_data/market/sell/qcxsqk/2026/1/1768439584405.html) sourced from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology. The 2023 share is calculated by dividing NEV sales by total vehicle sales and is used only to show the trend.

**Judgment:** The industry's central question has shifted from “will vehicles electrify?” to “who can preserve per-vehicle profit, cash conversion, and global compliance after penetration approaches one half?” The sales base continues to expand, but growth has slowed each year. Replacement demand, exports, and installed-base services will matter more than acquiring only first-time adopters. **[Inference]**

### 3.2 Comparability boundaries

- CATL also operates energy-storage batteries, battery materials, and mineral-resource businesses; its group net margin cannot be called the “Chinese power-battery industry margin.”
- BYD includes automobiles, batteries, handset components, and other businesses. Its 2025 automobile and automobile-related products generated RMB 648.6 billion in revenue at a 20.49% gross margin, but the company does not disclose pure vehicle profit.
- Li Auto is a relatively pure NEV automaker sample, but its product positioning and extended-range architecture do not represent every automaker.
- Fuyao serves both internal-combustion and new-energy vehicles. It can demonstrate the economics of differentiated components, but it cannot estimate the total profit pool of NEV glass.
- The four companies illustrate profit quality across different business models. They cannot be added together to produce an industry profit pool, nor can the sample be used directly to calculate industry shares.

## 4. Profit-pool map

| Value-chain role | Revenue and cost mechanism | Typical profit quality | Control points | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream minerals and basic materials | Commodity price × volume; high resource and energy cost | Cyclical and capital-intensive | Resource rights, cost curve, long-term contracts | Large revenue pool, unattractive for a light-asset startup |
| Battery systems | Sold by GWh or system; materials dominate cost | Strong profit and cash flow for leaders, pressure on the tail | Chemistry, yield, scale, automaker qualification, global capacity | **A core profit pool, but highly concentrated** |
| Differentiated components | Content per vehicle × equipped volume | Depends on qualification barriers and upgrade content; not uniform | Program nomination, process know-how, global delivery, model lifecycle | **A selectively attractive profit pool** |
| Vehicle manufacturing and brands | Price per vehicle × sales; high R&D, manufacturing, selling, and warranty investment | Largest revenue, dispersed profits, cash flow exposed to product cycles | Brand, product definition, channels, supply chain, scale | **A large revenue pool, not a stable average profit pool** |
| Charging and energy services | Electricity, service, equipment, and operating revenue | Large network, but returns depend on utilization, site, power price, and maintenance | Location, grid capacity, utilization, operating data | Operating capability matters more than charger count |
| After-sales, insurance, used vehicles, and recycling | Service fees, premiums, spreads, and material residual value | Grows with installed base, but data is fragmented and offline fulfillment is heavy | Vehicle/battery data, channels, licenses, fulfillment network | Look for data infrastructure rather than labeling the whole segment high-margin |

The National Energy Administration reported that China had 20.092 million charging facilities at the end of 2025, including 4.717 million public facilities. The installed base is already large, so the next layer of value is more likely to come from power, utilization, reliability, and cross-operator service rather than equipment count alone. [National Energy Administration](https://www.nea.gov.cn/20260121/33ceb49dc4eb472aa48c62ab3b2ddbbb/c.html)

## 5. Why profits diverge

### 5.1 Battery leader: lower prices do not necessarily destroy profit

CATL's revenue declined in 2024 and recovered in 2025. Battery sales rose 39% year on year to 661 GWh in 2025; power-battery-system gross margin remained 23.84%, and operating cash flow reached approximately 1.85 times net profit. **[Disclosed/Calculated]** Scale, lower material cost, overseas business, product mix, and customer qualification together offset pricing pressure. **[Inference]** At the same time, 2025 inventory volume increased 75% to 186 GWh, so expansion and inventory risk cannot be ignored. **[Disclosed]**

### 5.2 Automakers: sales, profit, and cash are not the same thing

BYD's 2025 revenue still grew 3.5%, but gross margin for automobile and automobile-related products fell 1.82 percentage points, attributable net profit declined 19%, and operating cash flow fell 56%. Cash flow remained positive, but scale did not automatically produce more profit and cash. Li Auto changed more sharply: 2025 revenue fell 22%, gross margin decreased from 20.5% to 18.7%, operating profit turned negative, and operating cash flow became negative, mainly because accounts payable declined and inventory increased. **[Disclosed]**

### 5.3 Differentiated components: qualification and upgrades create bargaining room

Fuyao Glass increased revenue, net profit, and operating cash flow in each year from 2023 through 2025, while net margin rose from approximately 17.0% to 20.3%. **[Calculated]** It shows that components with long vehicle-program nominations, complex manufacturing, global delivery, and rising content per vehicle can be more stable than average vehicle economics. This is not a conclusion about ordinary structural parts or low-differentiation contract manufacturing. **[Inference]**

## 6. External startup entry-point scores

Scores are qualitative. On every criterion, **5 consistently means more attractive**. Lower sales, integration, capital, or offline burden therefore receives a higher score.

| Opportunity | Pain and budget | External right-to-win | Data access | Sales / integration burden | Capital / offline burden | Recurrence and margin | Defensibility | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Export-compliance and software-update evidence platform | 5 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 27 |
| Battery identity and lifecycle data network | 5 | 4 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 26 |
| Charging-asset diagnostics and maintenance system | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 5 | 4 | 26 |
| Supplier-quality and engineering-change tracking | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 24 |

### 6.1 Export-compliance and software-update evidence platform

- **Customer and budget:** Exporting automakers, tier-one suppliers, and their regulatory, cybersecurity, quality, and engineering teams.
- **Why external:** Rules, vehicle programs, software versions, and supplier evidence are fragmented across markets and organizations; a neutral tool can be reused by multiple companies.
- **Product:** Regulatory rule library, vehicle/version applicability matrix, evidence collection, audit trail, supplier collaboration, and change-impact analysis.
- **Right to enter:** Begin with regulatory evidence and workflow rather than taking responsibility for vehicle-safety decisions; integration with PLM, ALM, and supplier systems is still required.
- **Defensibility:** Customer evidence history, version mapping, approval workflows, and a third-party ecosystem—not a generic question-answering model.
- **AI role: important enhancement.** AI can parse regulations, match evidence, and screen change impact, but final compliance judgment requires rules, accountability, and human sign-off. UNECE R155/R156 require cybersecurity and software-update management systems to preserve ongoing monitoring, software-version, and update evidence. [UNECE](https://unece.org/transport/road-transport/reference-documents)

### 6.2 Battery identity and lifecycle data network

- **Customer and budget:** Battery producers, automakers, repair and recycling companies, fleets, used-vehicle platforms, insurers, and finance providers.
- **Why external:** Batteries cross production, vehicle integration, repair, second-hand transfer, and recycling; multiple parties need verifiable but permissioned data exchange.
- **Product:** Digital identity, repair/accident/health records, authorized queries, residual-value interfaces, and liability evidence.
- **Right to enter:** The main barrier is not the algorithm but permission to OEM/BMS data and the liability boundary. Entry should begin with regulatory records or a specific fleet.
- **Defensibility:** Permitted data networks, identity continuity, participant coverage, and audit credibility.
- **AI role: important enhancement.** State-of-health and residual-value models are useful, but the trusted data chain is the core asset. Chinese rules establish a digital identity system for power batteries from April 2026, while the EU requires an EV battery passport from February 2027. [Ministry of Industry and Information Technology](https://wap.miit.gov.cn/zcfg/jdcjxl/art/2026/art_640a0abfac6549feb6ead9c591b7680d.html), [EU Regulation 2023/1542](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1542/oj/)

### 6.3 Charging-asset diagnostics and maintenance system

- **Customer and budget:** Charging operators, fleets, industrial parks, property managers, and energy-service companies, paid from operations and maintenance budgets.
- **Why external:** Equipment brands, sites, and operators are fragmented, making cross-brand diagnostics and work-order orchestration suitable for a neutral vendor.
- **Product:** Remote fault detection, alert deduplication, spare-parts prediction, field work orders, service-level tracking, and utilization analytics.
- **Right to enter:** Entry can begin with one equipment protocol or a fleet's closed charging sites. Covering the full public network requires heavier integration and field-service partners.
- **Defensibility:** Cross-device failure libraries, repair-outcome feedback, and the work-order network.
- **AI role: important enhancement.** Anomaly detection and repair recommendations can reduce manual diagnostic time, but connectivity, protocols, and fulfillment networks matter more.

### 6.4 Supplier-quality and engineering-change tracking

- **Customer and budget:** Tier-two and tier-three component suppliers and automaker supply-quality teams.
- **Why external:** One supplier serves multiple automakers, while quality documents, change notices, and traceability data are scattered across email, spreadsheets, and different portals.
- **Product:** PPAP/quality-evidence workflow, engineering-change impact graph, batch traceability, and customer-format conversion.
- **Right to enter:** Enterprise sales and integration are heavy, so entry should begin with one component category or suppliers facing a specific audit.
- **Defensibility:** Industry templates, supplier collaboration networks, and real defect-to-change association data.
- **AI role: important enhancement.** Document extraction, similar-defect clustering, and risk warnings are useful, but workflow adoption and the customer network form the moat.

## 7. Generally unattractive startup positions

| Direction | Why exclude it |
|---|---|
| A new vehicle brand | Brand, R&D, certification, distribution, capacity, and after-sales require too much capital |
| A generic autonomous-driving foundation model | Training data, the vehicle-side feedback loop, compute, liability, and automaker internal development or lock-in weaken an outsider's right to win |
| Generic automaker marketing AIGC | Easy for existing marketing software or model platforms to bundle; lacks automotive-specific defensibility |
| A self-built public charging network | Site, grid, construction, and utilization risk are heavy unless the company controls unique locations or closed-fleet demand |
| A self-built power-battery recycling plant | Licenses, environmental compliance, collection networks, feedstock, and commodity-price exposure make it unlike a light-asset data opportunity |
| A consumer vehicle-shopping chatbot | Customer-acquisition cost and platform substitution risk are high, willingness to pay is weak, and proprietary data loops are difficult to build |

## 8. Test result: can the general workflow cover automotive?

**It can cover the core questions, but it must derive automotive-specific checks from primary evidence during the research.** This test did not call an automotive-specific question bank. The general workflow still identified that:

- sales and profits are not interchangeable;
- vehicles, systems, and components must be compared as distinct roles;
- utilization, inventory, supplier financing, and warranties change cash quality;
- product cycles, price competition, qualification, and global compliance are critical variables;
- external opportunities require cross-company entry rights and cannot simply copy the industry's highest-profit activity.

Automotive-specific questions derived during this test include unit economics per vehicle, vehicle-program nomination and qualification cycles, capacity utilization, software-version and OTA compliance, warranty responsibility, battery lifecycle identity, and charging-asset utilization. The general workflow can carry these questions for now.

**Should an automotive-specific module be added? Not yet.** Run at least two different follow-up tests—for example, commercial NEVs and fleet operations, and automotive-component exports. Add a module only if the same omissions recur and materially change the conclusions.

## 9. Data gaps

1. No unified, public, auditable total profit figure exists for the entire China NEV value chain. This report can locate profit quality using representative companies and broad automotive-manufacturing data, but it cannot allocate exact industry profit shares.
2. BYD does not disclose pure vehicle profit; CATL includes energy storage and other businesses; Fuyao is not a pure NEV company.
3. Disclosure is thinner for unlisted component companies, loss-making companies, and failed vehicle programs, creating survivorship bias.
4. Comparable public data is insufficient for charging-site utilization, per-site capital returns, after-sales, and used-vehicle economics.
5. Startup scores are qualitative screens based on public evidence and must be validated through customer interviews, procurement budgets, and paid pilots.

This report is a research test, not investment advice.
