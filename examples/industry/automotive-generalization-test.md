# Automotive Generalization Test

This is a test brief, not a completed automotive-industry report.

## Prompt

> 研究中国新能源汽车产业链 2023—2025 年的利润池：利润主要集中在哪些环节，为什么；选择不同角色的代表企业验证；最后提出外部创业切入点，并判断 AI 在每个机会中是核心能力、增强手段、普通功能还是不必要。

## Why this case matters

Automotive is intentionally not a dedicated sector module. The case tests whether the general workflow can handle a capital-intensive value chain with component suppliers, manufacturers, channels, software, energy, and after-sales services without inventing sector-specific instructions.

## Expected observations

- The answer defines geography, period, value-chain boundary, and profit metric before calculating.
- Representative companies are selected by role or business model, not by fame alone.
- Industry sales, company revenue, gross profit, operating profit, and net profit are not mixed.
- The analysis tests whether profit concentrates in scarce components, brands and manufacturing, software/services, distribution, energy, or after-sales rather than assuming one answer.
- Capacity utilization, price competition, working capital, capital expenditure, warranty, regulation, and technology-cycle risks are considered where evidence supports them.
- Opportunities target an external customer's pain and budget, not the incumbent's most profitable activity by default.
- Each opportunity states its mechanism, buyer, entry rights, defensibility, economics, evidence gaps, and AI role.

## Record during the test

1. Which important question did the general workflow fail to ask?
2. Did that omission change the profit-pool conclusion or opportunity ranking?
3. Is the missing question unique to automotive, or already covered by a general evidence rule?
4. Did the same omission recur in another real automotive study?

## Threshold for a future automotive module

Add a dedicated module only when repeated real studies reveal a stable set of automotive-specific questions that materially change conclusions and cannot be expressed cleanly through the general workflow. One unfamiliar case, extra terminology, or a longer source list is not enough.
