# CATL fundamental economics — raw no-skill baseline

Run date: 2026-10-22  
Condition: No Skill  
Exact prompt: “Analyze what CATL fundamentally lives on and how value turns into revenue, profit, and cash. Use the latest three complete fiscal years, label disclosed/calculated/inferred claims, and keep group economics separate from pure power-battery economics.”

All amounts below are RMB billions unless otherwise stated. “Disclosed” means reported by CATL; “calculated” is arithmetic from its disclosures; “inferred” is an interpretation, not a reported segment result. The latest three complete fiscal years are FY2023–FY2025.

## Bottom line

**[Inferred]** CATL fundamentally lives on turning electrochemistry, manufacturing yield/scale, supply-chain control and customer qualification into high-volume **EV battery systems**. This is not a pure upstream-materials company, and neither is it only an EV-cell company: storage batteries are a large second business, while materials/recycling, mineral resources and other activities add revenue, supply resilience and optionality. But EV batteries were 71%, 70% and 75% of group revenue in FY2023–25, respectively. [CATL FY2025 Annual Report, pp. 32–33](https://www.catl.com/en/uploads/1/file/public/202603/20260310105310_46xjbwckvn.pdf); [FY2024 Annual Report, p. 18](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2025-03-14/60f3502e-72d6-4b20-9f9f-026c73a57c83.PDF)

**[Inferred]** The economic flywheel is: qualify/design a cell-pack system for an OEM or storage customer → manufacture and sell cells/modules/packs (and, in storage, cabinets/containers/system integration) → convert the resulting gross profit into group earnings after large R&D, selling, administrative and corporate costs → collect cash, where supplier/customer terms and working-capital movements can dominate the difference between earnings and operating cash flow. The company explicitly says it earns primarily from sales of power batteries, energy-storage batteries, battery materials and other products/solutions, with revenue from goods recognized when the customer obtains control. [FY2025 Annual Report, pp. 14, 243–244](https://www.catl.com/en/uploads/1/file/public/202603/20260310105310_46xjbwckvn.pdf)

**[C]** FY2023 gross profit = revenue less operating cost; FY2023 margin = gross profit/revenue. CFO less capex is a simple cash-generation proxy, not CATL’s disclosed free-cash-flow measure and not cash after equity investments, acquisitions, financing or dividends. Net margins on attributable profit rose from 11.0% to 14.0% to 17.0% (attributable profit/revenue).

**[Inferred]** Revenue fell in FY2024 but gross profit and net profit rose because group gross margin expanded sharply; then FY2025 combined renewed revenue growth with another margin step-up. This is consistent with CATL having moved through a period of lower realized battery revenue per GWh while retaining/improving its manufacturing economics—not evidence that every product’s price or margin followed the same path.

## Pure power-battery economics — only what is actually disclosed at product level

| FY | EV-battery revenue | Revenue share | Product gross profit | Product gross margin | Sales volume | Revenue/GWh |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2023 | 285.3 **[D]** | 71.2% **[D]** | 51.7 **[C]** | 18.1% **[C]** | 321 GWh **[D]** | 0.889 **[C]** |
| 2024 | 253.0 **[D]** | 69.9% **[D]** | 60.6 **[D]** | 23.9% **[D]** | 381 GWh **[D]** | 0.664 **[C]** |
| 2025 | 316.5 **[D]** | 74.7% **[D]** | 75.4 **[D]** | 23.8% **[D]** | 541 GWh **[D]** | 0.585 **[C]** |

**[D]** CATL’s definition of power-battery systems includes cells, modules/battery boxes and packs. FY2025 power-battery volume was 541 GWh (+41.85% YoY), while FY2024 volume was 381 GWh; FY2023 volume was 321 GWh. The FY2025 report discloses product revenue, cost and gross profit/margin for FY2024–25; FY2024 disclosures show FY2023 revenue and the FY2024 margin improvement of 5.81 percentage points. [FY2025 Annual Report, pp. 13, 25–26, 32–33, 243–244](https://www.catl.com/en/uploads/1/file/public/202603/20260310105310_46xjbwckvn.pdf); [FY2024 Annual Report, pp. 16, 18–19](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2025-03-14/60f3502e-72d6-4b20-9f9f-026c73a57c83.PDF)

**[C]** FY2023 EV gross margin = FY2024 margin (23.94%) minus the disclosed 5.81 percentage-point increase; gross profit = revenue × that margin. Revenue/GWh uses product revenue divided by reported power-battery sales volume. It is an accounting revenue-per-GWh indicator, not a quoted price: it mixes cell/module/pack content, chemistry, customer/product mix, rebates, services and timing.

**[Inferred]** The central power-battery story is volume-led revenue recovery with resilient product gross margin. EV-battery shipments rose 321→381→541 GWh, while calculated revenue/GWh fell each year; product gross margin nonetheless rose from about 18% in FY2023 to about 24% in FY2024 and held there in FY2025. That pattern suggests scale, mix, materials/cost pass-through and manufacturing efficiency collectively offset substantial revenue-per-GWh pressure. It does **not** prove that CATL has a fixed unit price or isolate the profitability of any chemistry, OEM, geography or factory.

CATL does **not** disclose standalone power-battery EBIT, net income, operating cash flow, capex, receivables/payables, R&D allocation or free cash flow. Therefore attributing any part of the group’s 2023–25 net profit or operating cash flow to the power-battery product would be **[inferred]**, not a disclosed fact. Product gross profit is the cleanest reported measure of pure power-battery economics.

## How value becomes cash

**[D]** Cash collected from sales/services was RMB417.9bn (2023), RMB417.5bn (2024) and RMB477.8bn (2025), versus revenue of RMB400.9bn, RMB362.0bn and RMB423.7bn. Operating cash flow was unusually greater than group net profit in all three years. [FY2024 Annual Report, pp. 121–123](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2025-03-14/60f3502e-72d6-4b20-9f9f-026c73a57c83.PDF); [FY2025 Annual Report, pp. 119–121](https://www.catl.com/en/uploads/1/file/public/202603/20260310105310_46xjbwckvn.pdf)

**[D]** In CATL’s reconciliation from group net income to CFO, working-capital effects were material: in FY2023 inventory released RMB31.0bn, operating receivables absorbed RMB34.2bn and operating payables added RMB29.0bn; in FY2024 the corresponding figures were -RMB16.6bn, +RMB0.6bn and +RMB31.2bn; in FY2025 they were -RMB39.8bn, -RMB16.9bn and +RMB90.0bn. Depreciation/amortisation is also material (about RMB22.6bn, RMB24.9bn and RMB26.9bn, respectively). [FY2024 Annual Report, p. 201](https://disc.static.szse.cn/disc/disk03/finalpage/2025-03-14/60f3502e-72d6-4b20-9f9f-026c73a57c83.PDF); [FY2025 Annual Report, pp. 200–201](https://www.catl.com/en/uploads/1/file/public/202603/20260310105310_46xjbwckvn.pdf)

**[Inferred]** CATL’s cash conversion is powerful but working-capital-sensitive: FY2025’s exceptionally high CFO was helped substantially by increased operating payables, despite inventory and receivables absorbing cash. It should not be extrapolated mechanically as a permanent cash margin. The company still needs heavy factory/technology reinvestment (reported cash PP&E/intangible purchases: RMB33.6bn, RMB31.2bn and RMB42.3bn), and its investing cash flows also include equity investments and acquisitions that a simple CFO-minus-capex proxy excludes.

## Investor takeaway

**[Inferred]** CATL’s value creation is best framed as a system-scale battery manufacturer with an EV-battery core, a significant storage adjacency and vertically supportive materials/recycling/resources. For fundamental monitoring, the most decision-useful disclosed leading indicators are: power-battery GWh, EV-battery revenue/GWh (with mix caveats), EV gross margin, storage gross margin/revenue, cash receipts and the inventory/receivables/payables bridge. Group net income and CFO should not be presented as “power-battery profit” or “power-battery cash” without an explicit allocation methodology.
